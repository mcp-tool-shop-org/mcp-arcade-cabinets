# Dispatch — Ghost on the Menu, endless: the seat is the boss, and a score

**The brief, as decisions (the Director, 2026-09-17).** Ghost on the Menu gets an endless mode with enough variance and a rising difficulty that it is fun, and a score. Endless is a **seated mode only**: the menu offers a model picker (the local daemon's tags, the Cloud tags, Claude by key through the launcher's tier) and the mode lights only when a seat answers, so the model drives the variance and is the boss; Pages never shows it. The score design is the coordinator's call, put here as decisions with the research behind each.

**What the lock already says, and the one line this amends.** G7 (the round ends with a scene, not a count; the field carries no digit), G8 (no ranking against other players), G10 (the end screen says which tape, server, policy and nothing more), G19–G22 (a shift is a seeded draw; lamps refill each call; the climb is data; four-word code). Endless keeps the field's rule whole: the field canvas never shows a digit and never a fact. It amends G10 and G20 for endless alone: an endless run's closing scene carries the run's score beside the tape list, because the score is the game's number and not the tape's, on the same ground Vibe Typer's G23 stands on. That amendment is the Director's word, given with this brief.

## Standing frame

The lead writes every public surface. Builders are Opus subagents in worktrees, one branch a slice from `main`, one commit each, a review from a packet cut from the merge base, `pnpm verify` and `pnpm build:play` green, `pnpm build:launcher` for both packages when packaging moves, identity scan clean before every push. **The player's pass** before every merge: the coordinator plays the built bundle with a seat lit and reads every line and every number on the field and the closing scene; a second reviewer of another family reads the transcript. Never quote the Director in a file. Feel numbers are `// Director` constants or JSON levers. The seat's words go through the say gate and the seat's picks through the fact-blind contract, as every seat already does (G11–G18); nothing the model says or chooses may carry a fact, a digit or a name onto the field.

## Research grounding

Four questions were sent to parallel research agents on 2026-09-17; each finding below is connected to the decision it bears on.

**How the climb should climb.** Csikszentmihalyi's flow channel as Schell applies it (Schell, _The Art of Game Design_, 2008, ch. 15; https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/understanding-the-flow-channel-in-game-design): challenge must track skill or the run reads as anxiety or boredom; so the climb is a curve, not a step per call. Galante on Vampire Survivors (NME, 2022; https://www.nme.com/features/vampire-survivors-creator-luca-galante-talks-quitting-his-job-to-fulfil-his-promise-3153107): density held low for an opening stretch before real pressure; so the first calls sit on a flat band. Yu, _Spelunky_ (Boss Fight Books, 2016): danger is spiky and situational, not a smoothly rising number; so the climb widens variance (more hazard kinds, more formation combinations) before it raises lethality. The NES Tetris kill screen (Tom's Hardware, 2024): an unbounded curve without a designed ceiling hits the input limit by accident and reads as broken; so `endless.json` carries an explicit asymptote per tier (max copies, max intensity). Galaga's Challenging Stages (Kokutech; https://www.kokutech.com/blog/gamedev/design-patterns/flow-state/galaga): no-return-fire bonus rounds as breathers inside the climb; so a breather call is scheduled by count, independent of the climb. Hunicke and Chapman, "AI for Dynamic Difficulty Adjustment in Games" (2004; https://users.cs.northwestern.edu/~hunicke/pubs/Hamlet.pdf): players resent difficulty they can see being lowered; so the seat's mercy after a bad call is a gentler flavor from the menu, never a softened stat. Hegedues et al., "Investigating Perceived and Mechanical Challenge in Games Through Cognitive Activity" (arXiv:2307.03524, 2023): unflagged recurrence at higher intensity erodes trust; so when a tape comes back harder the boss says so, through the gate.

**Variance from a small pool.** Gravina, Khalifa, Liapis, Togelius, Yannakakis, "Procedural Content Generation through Quality Diversity" (arXiv:1907.04053, 2019): a generator that optimizes quality alone collapses onto the same few outputs; so the menu the cabinet hands the seat is built to cover the feature space (flavor × density band × tier) against what the run has seen, and the seat picks within it. Smith and Whitehead, "Analyzing the Expressive Range of a Level Generator" (PCG Workshop, 2010; https://users.soe.ucsc.edu/~ejw/papers/smith-pcg-2010.pdf): plot what the generator produces over many seeds before shipping; so the band prints the flavor-by-call and tape-by-call spread of the scripted seat over a hundred runs and fails on a hole. Bénard on Dead Cells (GDC 2019; https://gdcvault.com/play/1025788): novelty gated by progress reads as discovery; so the early band draws from a narrower slice of the roster and the pool widens with the climb. Spelunky's seeded daily and its farming problem (PC Gamer, 2013; Yu, GDC Vault 1027187): a fixed order gets memorized; so the code replays the seed and the difficulty, and the record says plainly that the same code meets a different boss.

**What the score measures.** Ryan, Rigby and Przybylski, "The Motivational Pull of Video Games: A Self-Determination Theory Approach" (_Motivation and Emotion_ 30(4), 2006): the score works as competence feedback and collapses when it does not map to felt skill; and no cited system scores elapsed time; so time is not scored, and lasting long pays only because later calls pay more. DoDonPachi's chain (Shmups Wiki; https://shmups.wiki/library/DoDonPachi) and Ikaruga's polarity chain (PC Gamer; https://www.pcgamer.com/ikarugas-rule-of-threes/): a discrete multiplier that resets to a floor on a mistake is legible where a decaying one is not; so the chain is whole links and resets to one on a lamp lost. Galaga and Space Invaders tiered values (Prime Time Amusements): points encode risk; so a boss put down and a drop caught pay their own lines, disproportionately. Geometry Wars' geoms (Wikipedia): the multiplier is collected in risky space; so drops keep falling where the fire is. Fumoto on Downwell (GDC 2016; https://gdcvault.com/play/1023533; Game Developer analysis): the combo is tuned to reward a clean read-and-stop, with a cash-out; so the call's end banks the chain's value. Tetris Guideline scoring (https://tetris.wiki/Scoring): bonuses named per technique teach what the game values; so the closing scene shows three lines, catches, bosses, calls, not one number alone. Na and Han, "How leaderboard positions shape our motivation" (_Internet Research_ 33(7), 2023; doi:10.1108/INTR-12-2021-0897): rank against others motivates the top half and frustrates the rest; with G8, the best is this browser's own and nothing is sent.

**Where the number lives.** Kao, Ballou, Gerling, Breitsohl, Deterding, "How does Juicy Game Feedback Motivate?" (CHI 2024; https://doi.org/10.1145/3613904.3642656): feedback tied to skill-dependent success raises competence; decoration lowers it; so every pop is event-gated. Kao et al., "The Effects of Juiciness in an Action RPG" (_Entertainment Computing_, 2020): medium juice beats none and beats extreme; Begy, "Good Feedback for Bad Players?": juice lowered objective scores in an action task; so in-run feedback stays light and the celebration is the closing scene. Burgers et al., "How Feedback Boosts Motivation and Play in a Brain-Training Game" (_Computers in Human Behavior_, 2015; https://doi.org/10.1016/j.chb.2015.01.038): evaluative feedback sustains play better than comparative; so the closing scene names the run in a word before it shows the number. Immediate versus delayed feedback (Frontiers in Psychology, 2021; PMC8696276): a run with no live signal loses real-time reinforcement; so the bezel carries the chain as a word during play, and the digits wait for the scene. Jonasson and Purho, "Juice It or Lose It" (GDC Europe 2012): the pop, the chime and the shake are separable from the digit; so the field gets the catch's feedback without a number.

## The lock, G31 to G35

**G31. Endless is a seated mode.** It lights on the menu only when a seat answers a probe: a tag on the local daemon, a Cloud tag, or Claude by key through the launcher's tier; the picker sits on the menu outside the field (G17) and the chosen tag is never on the field. Pages carries no endless. A seat that stops answering mid-run hands the boss to the seeded fallback and the bezel says so in words; the run does not end for it. (G11, G17.)

**G32. The seat is the boss.** At every call the cabinet builds a menu the seat picks from through the fact-blind contract: three candidate tapes named by header only, chosen to cover what the run has not seen (flavor, density band, tier); the flavor for the call; and a tell, one line through the say gate, said as the call opens. The seat also fires and speaks as the seats already do. A pick off the menu, late, or refused falls to the seeded pick and is counted in the run's record. Nothing the seat picks or says is a fact, a digit or a name (G1, G11–G18). The same code meets a different boss, and the closing scene says so.

**G33. The climb is data with a floor, a curve and a ceiling.** `endless.json` holds an opening band of calls on the tape-alone reach, a per-call step, and an asymptote per tier (max copies, max intensity, max concurrent hazards) the climb never crosses. Up the climb the menu widens (more flavors, more of the roster, formation combinations) before the reach rises. A breather call is scheduled by count, independent of the climb. The tape-alone bars and the shift bar stand as they are; the endless band is its own: at tier zero the scripted seat survives a stated number of calls, the live mover fewer, and the spread of flavors and tapes over a hundred seeded runs has no hole. (G21.)

**G34. Lamps are a life pool in endless.** One lamp comes back on a clean call (none lost); none refill otherwise; the run ends at the last lamp with the tape's own scene, then the tally. (Amends G20 for endless alone.)

**G35. The score is the game's number, never the tape's.** A catch pays a base times the chain; the chain is whole links, one per catch or drop while no lamp is lost, capped by a lever, and resets to one on a lamp lost; a boss put down pays its own line times the chain; a drop caught pays a small line and links the chain; the call's end banks the chain's value with a call bonus that rises with the reach. Time is not scored. The field canvas carries no digit and no fact (G7). The bezel carries the lamps and the chain as a word, event-gated, light. The closing scene carries the run's word (from a rank table on the banked score per tier), the three lines (catches, bosses, calls), the score, and this browser's best in `localStorage`, never sent anywhere (G8, G10 amended for endless). The code is four words plus the difficulty and replays the draw, not the seat's choices (G22).

## Data levers (`packages/ghost-on-the-menu/patterns/endless.json`)

- `climb`: `{ openingCalls, stepPerCall, ceiling: { copies, intensity, hazards } per tier }`.
- `breatherEvery`: calls between breather calls.
- `menu`: `{ candidates: 3, widenAt: [call indices at which the roster slice and the flavor list widen] }`.
- `lamps`: `{ backOnCleanCall: 1 }`.
- `score`: `{ catch, drop, boss, callBonusBase, chainCap }` and `rank`: the word table per tier (banked score thresholds → a word).
- `seat`: `{ timeoutMs, fallback: 'seeded' }`.
- The chain words and the rank words are in the levers so the lead can rewrite them as content.
